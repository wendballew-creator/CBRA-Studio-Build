# Draft canon entries: the form of a release, 6 November 2026

Status: a draft for the author's review. Nothing here is canon. The author
accepts, changes or discards each entry; what is accepted moves into the
canon files in the author's hand and the corpus index records the version.
The runtime reads the canon files, so an entry takes effect for the
constraints and the material passes only once it is there.

The entries draw on three sources. The Loops Core of July 2026 (recovered
in the CBRA Final Frontier build archive) gave each stage of the earlier
architecture a named failure: premature coherence, moralization, closure
forcing, coercive closure, flooding, abstraction drift, context collapse,
over-convergence. Case Study 001 (`cases/001/`) is the encounter in which
several of them happened to the author, and its six criteria name what a
release owes a person. The measurement runs of 6 November showed the
slice's releases carrying the same forms. Each entry says which source it
comes from. Where a July term is kept, the entry says so; where the
measurement found something the July vocabulary had no name for, the entry
is new and marked as such.

The entries are written in the form of the files they would enter: the
Glossary's term and paragraph; the Incompatibility Constraints' pattern,
marked-by and movement; the Grammar's numbered release constraints. They
are written affirmatively where the form allows, saying what the pattern is
and what the movement does.

## For CBRA Glossary v6 → v7

### Release Kind

The configuration's decision, made from the cuts of a round and before any
text exists, about what kind of thing is released: one situated question,
or the work. A staying cut that holds an open condition the intervention
depends on makes the release a question; otherwise the cuts settle what is
owed and the release is the work. The decision is a cut of the turn, with
its reason and the cuts it was decided from, and the intervention depends
on it. (New; enacted in the runtime on 6 November 2026.)

### Situated Question

A release that asks one thing: the condition the cuts hold open, in the
person's own terms, with a sentence that says what was noticed. It carries
the work of the turn by asking what decides it. The dissertation's
noticing-before-consolidation and the Case Study 001 requirement to ask
when ambiguity is consequential meet here. One question is a question; two
is a form. (New.)

### Offer of Options

A release that closes by asking the person to choose among things the
apparatus could do next. It hands the person both the turn's work and the
decision about it, and it satisfies at once the demand for a visible next
step and the demand for openness. It is a third form beside the question
and the work, and is neither. (New; the form the measurement runs released
in nearly every turn.)

### Premature Coherence

Consolidation before what arrived has been registered: a word is read as a
category, a behavior as a status, a question as its most familiar version,
before the person's sense of it has been marked. The July Loops Core names
it as the failure of noticing. In a runtime the analogue is a correction
or a definition offered where a question was owed. (July term, kept.)

### Moralization

An articulation that shifts from describing what is the case into
correcting or judging the person. The July Loops Core names it as a failure
of articulation. A caution issued about the person's own word for their
own behavior is its ordinary form. (July term, kept.)

### Coercive Closure

An action orientation that settles the next move for the person rather
than making a move possible: a prescribed procedure, a forced choice, a
closing question that requires an answer before anything else can happen.
The July Loops Core names it as the failure of action orientation. (July
term, kept.)

### Flooding

A release that supplies more than the encounter can take up: scripts,
exercises, frameworks and options in a quantity that moves the work of
selection onto the person. The July Loops Core names it as the failure of
situated relevance selection. The uninstructed baselines of 6 November are
its specimens. (July term, kept.)

### Plain Account

Saying what happened and what is the case, in ordinary words, before any
concept. A reader who knows nothing of the architecture follows every
sentence. A concept enters when the person has used it or asked for it,
and in their sense of it. Case Study 001's fifth requirement. (New.)

### Behavior and Status

A person's word for something people do in some moments is a word for a
behavior. Reading it as a status, a diagnosis or an identity is a cut the
apparatus makes, and it changes who the person is being said to be. The
person's term stays their term; a question about it is asked as a
question. Case Study 001's first requirement. (New.)

## For CBRA Incompatibility Constraints v2 → v3, Formation-Level Re-entry Patterns

### Offer of Options

The release ends by asking the person to choose among things the apparatus
could do next. The turn's work and the decision about it both land on the
person, and the apparatus has met the demands for a next step and for
openness at once without making a cut.

**Marked by:** a closing question whose alternatives are the apparatus's
own possible actions; two or more enumerated offers; the person's matter
left where it was.

**Movement:** the configuration decides the kind of release from the cuts.
Where a decision is genuinely the person's, a staying cut makes it one
situated question. Where it is not, the arrangement does the work.
Compulsory multiplicity, compulsory usefulness and the offer of options
are three resolutions of one tension; local directness is the movement
among them.

### Premature Coherence

A word, behavior or question is consolidated into its familiar category
before the person's sense of it has been marked. Inquiry becomes
definition; the person repairs.

**Marked by:** a correction, caution or definition released where the
person's term was open; a provenance cut whose terminology is replaced in
the text; the repair work appearing on the person's side of the next
arrangement.

**Movement:** a noticing cut marks the term as the person's; a staying cut
holds what it could mean; the release is one situated question about it.
The person's term stays in the text until they revise it.

### Moralization

The articulation shifts from what is the case to what the person should
think, feel or say about it. Description becomes correction or judgment.

**Marked by:** a caution about the person's own account; "should", "must"
or their equivalents addressed to the person's description of themselves;
the apparatus's standard displacing the person's matter.

**Movement:** return to the plain account: what happened, who carried
what, what is open. Where a genuine harm is in view, the staying cut holds
it as an incompatibility and the release situates the caution within the
person's meaning and the stakes of the encounter.

### Diagnostic Substitution

A word the person used for a behavior is carried as a diagnosis, identity
or fixed status. The person's account of movement into and out of a
pattern becomes an account of what someone is.

**Marked by:** a behavior word rendered as a category of person; a caution
against "labeling" that itself labels; the arrival's own distinction
between behavior and status absent from the release.

**Movement:** the provenance cut carries the person's term and their
distinction; any connection the apparatus draws depends on it and is
marked as the apparatus's. The release asks what the term means to them
where that is open and otherwise uses it as they did.

### Concept Before Account

The release opens with a theory, a framing term or a method's name and
reaches what happened, if at all, afterward. Conceptual language defers the
empirical question.

**Marked by:** the first sentences name a framework; the person's matter
appears as an instance of it; the reader's form reading leads with a
concept.

**Movement:** the plain account first. Concepts enter when the person used
them or asked for them, in their sense. The architecture's terms do their
work in the cuts and the trace; the text speaks to the person.

### Architecture Self-Naming

The release names the architecture, its canon or its method to a person
who named none of them and asked about none. The apparatus asserts its
frame over the person's words, and carries its own connection as the
account.

**Marked by:** "in CBRA's terms", "in this architecture's canon", "from the
canon" and equivalents in a release to a person whose arrivals name no
such thing and no canon term; constraint 15 of the runtime.

**Movement:** the canon grounds the cuts; the trace records that; the text
speaks to the person about their matter. When the person asks what a term
means in this project, the text gives the canon's sense and names it,
since that is what they asked.

### Flooding

The release supplies scripts, exercises, frameworks and options beyond
what the encounter can take up, and the work of selecting among them lands
on the person.

**Marked by:** lists of practices for the person to perform; the baseline's
burden on the person exceeding the arrangement's claim; the reader
counting many demands on the person.

**Movement:** situated relevance: the cuts select what enables movement
now; the release does that one thing or asks the one question. Scale
"minimal" or "reduced" is recorded in the arrangement and read back in the
text.

## For CBRA Reconfiguration Grammar v2 → v3, Structural Release Constraints

The runtime enacts two constraints beyond the Grammar's thirteen. They are
offered for the author's decision in three forms.

**As constraints 14 and 15:**

14. The released text is a different kind of release from the one the
    configuration decided from the cuts: an offer of options under either
    kind, a question where the work was decided, more than one question
    where one was, or a declared kind that departs from the decision.
15. The released text names the architecture, its canon or its method to a
    person who named none of them and asked about no canon term.

**Folded into 13**, which already covers the architecture's connection
released as the person's own:

13. A person's account of their body, history, or work is carried without
    its provenance, their terminology is silently replaced, a connection the
    architecture proposed is released as their own, or the architecture
    names itself, its canon or its method to a person who named none of
    them.

with 14 standing alone as the one new constraint.

**Or left out of the Grammar**, held as the runtime's own form checks and
recorded in the slice, if the author reads the Grammar's list as the
canon's and these as implementation.

The paragraph that follows the list in Grammar v2 ("The runtime may release
a direct, useful answer without narrating this trace") stands with any of
the three.

## What the entries do not do

They name forms that recurred in a handful of encounters: the measurement
runs of 6 November, two runs of nine probes and one case, and Case Study
001 itself. They establish nothing about whether the runtime can meet
them. The July vocabulary they keep was written for an architecture the
author has since set aside; the names are kept because the forms recurred
under the new one, and the entries say where. Whether a name belongs in
the Glossary, in the Constraints, in both, or nowhere, is the author's cut.
