# Recursive Feedback Re-entry and Further Effects Slice

## Bounded claim

A projection recomputed through later feedback may re-enter diffraction and
receive further material effects when its exact structural lineage remains
operative.  Neither the length nor ordering of recursive history supplies
maturity, confidence, or authority.

## Implemented boundary

- A current feedback-recomputed projection may act as a diffractive witness.
- Re-entry validates every exact originating recomputation back to the
  oscillation-grounded field return.
- Each link must retain its observation, disturbance delta, reopened
  formation, counter-cut lineage, recomputed trace, and current dependency.
- A later observation may confirm, complicate, or invalidate the newest
  recomputation.
- Complication may reopen a named local dependent while leaving the newest
  counter-cut active.
- Invalidation may reopen the newest counter-cut without judging either the
  counter-cut or prior confirmation as failure.
- All earlier feedback, revisions, displaced projections, and disturbances
  remain available as situated history.

## Excluded authority

The transaction has no maturity, seniority, depth, generation, iteration,
confidence, weight, score, rank, preference, winner, finality, success,
failure, chooser, or selection state.  Tuple order and history length are not
inputs to validation.

## Adversarial boundary

- Removing an earlier originating recomputation invalidates re-entry even
  when the latest revision remains present.
- Reversing the revision-history tuple does not change valid authority.
- Confirmation does not protect the newest counter-cut.
- Later complication preserves confirmation and the full recursive history.
- Later invalidation reopens the newest counter-cut without retroactive
  failure.
- Rejected re-entry leaves substrate state unchanged.
