# Counter-Cut Arrangement Revision Slice

## Boundary

This pass begins after a difference in one retained arrangement has reopened
the enabling formation of another. It adds the smallest mechanism that can:

- enact an explicit counter-cut for that invalidated enabling formation;
- recompute the affected arrangement's burden, capacity, consequence, power,
  and possibility trace;
- retain the prior proposal, disturbance, invalidation, counter-cut, and new
  proposal as one inspectable lineage; and
- keep the field free of scoring, ranking, preference, and selection.

It does not originate a counter-cut from language, determine that a revision
is caring, choose an arrangement, enact a public intervention, or observe an
external effect.

## Operative movement

The Case 2 fixture begins with two retained burden-sharing proposals. A lower
maximum local burden in the distributed proposal reopens the enabling cut of
the shared proposal and recursively invalidates its actionability and release
condition.

This pass then requires a counter-cut that:

1. preserves lineage to the exact invalidated enabling cut;
2. remains dependent on the active Case 2 source cut;
3. has a structural shape different from the invalidated enabling formation;
4. is enacted in the substrate under a new delta; and
5. explicitly enables a newly identified, recomputed arrangement.

The stale shared proposal had a maximum local burden of `2.0`. The recomputed
proposal has a maximum local burden of `1.5`, with allocation topology distinct
from both the stale shared proposal and the still-active distributed proposal.
This numeric difference is evidence of recomputation, not evidence that the
revision is better or should be selected.

## History preservation

Revision does not overwrite the disturbed arrangement. The current retained
projection substitutes the recomputed proposal at the disturbed proposal's
ordinal location, while an `ArrangementRevisionTrace` preserves:

- the disturbance delta;
- the reopened proposal;
- the invalidated enabling cut;
- the enacted counter-cut;
- the prior structural trace; and
- the recomputed structural trace.

The substrate separately retains every cut and delta. Invalidated cuts cease
to be active but remain recorded with the delta that invalidated them.

Ordinal continuity is not preference. The collection exposes no winner,
preferred flag, score, rank, chooser, or selection operation.

## Adversarial boundaries

The focused suite requires that:

- a disturbance must exactly match a delta recorded by the substrate;
- an enabling cut must already have been invalidated before it can receive a
  counter-cut;
- a renamed copy of the old enabling topology cannot count as a counter-cut;
- the counter-cut must name the reopened cut in its lineage;
- the counter-cut must depend on the active competing source cut;
- the recomputed arrangement must preserve lineage to the stale proposal;
- the recomputed arrangement must depend on the enacted counter-cut;
- a cosmetic recomputation with unchanged effects fails before substrate
  mutation;
- a recomputation duplicating another retained topology fails;
- the same disturbance cannot authorize repeated revisions; and
- rejected revisions leave substrate state unchanged.

## Claim limit and next boundary

This slice proves that a reopened arrangement can receive an explicit,
structurally distinct counter-cut and a freshly computed effect trace while
the full disturbance history remains available and no selector appears.

It does not prove that the revised arrangement should be enacted. The next
valid pass should allow a recomputed arrangement to re-enter diffractive
movement: its new effects may disturb other active enabling cuts, including a
counter-cut formed during an earlier revision, without permitting revision
depth, recency, or iteration count to become ranking authority. Model
generation, persistence integration, and public articulation remain outside
this boundary.
