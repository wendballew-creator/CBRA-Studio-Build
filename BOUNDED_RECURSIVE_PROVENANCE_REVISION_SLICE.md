# Bounded Recursive Provenance Revision Slice

## Boundary

This slice allows further complicating or invalidating effects on a recursively
feedback-recomputed projection to ground another explicit local counter-cut and
fresh recomputation. The operation preserves every feedback observation,
disturbance delta, displaced projection, counter-cut, and recomputed structural
trace. It does not turn the length of that history into maturity, confidence,
or authority.

The preceding runtime validated a feedback recomputation by recursively walking
every originating revision back to the oscillation-grounded field return. That
protected lineage, but it made an indefinitely expanding ancestry chain part of
the work required for present participation. This slice separates preserved
history from present validation.

## Bounded provenance closure

Each successfully preflighted feedback revision now forms a cumulative,
immutable `FeedbackProvenanceClosure`. The closure records:

- a mechanically derived continuity digest;
- the immediately preceding closure or oscillation-return origin;
- the exact observation and disturbance delta;
- the enacted counter-cut; and
- the newly recomputed alternative.

The closure is not a score, certificate of adequacy, or replacement for the
history. All underlying records remain in their existing collections. It is a
bounded continuity witness formed only after the ordinary structural,
substrate, counter-cut, and recomputation checks succeed.

A present feedback-recomputed formation is validated through:

- its exact current structural trace;
- its exact feedback-revision record;
- its recorded observation and substrate disturbance;
- its recorded and locally anchored counter-cut;
- its exact current closure; and
- one retained predecessor closure or the exact oscillation-return origin.

Validation does not recursively traverse earlier closures. Tuple position,
closure count, feedback depth, recency, and history length are not inputs.

## Further local revision

A further complicating effect may reopen only the explicitly named active
dependent formation. A further invalidating effect may reopen the current
enabling counter-cut. Either may ground a caller-proposed, structurally distinct
counter-cut and a freshly traced replacement for the affected current
projection. Unaffected competitors remain present.

The new counter-cut remains active and revisable. The closure does not protect
it, settle the field, or convert the new recomputation into a preferred result.

## Adversarial boundary

The tests establish that:

- further invalidation can ground a new counter-cut and recomputation;
- further complication remains local and leaves the enabling counter-cut active;
- complete feedback and revision history remains preserved beside the closures;
- a forged current closure fails without substrate mutation;
- removing the required predecessor closure fails;
- closure tuple ordering confers no authority;
- cosmetic recomputation fails before enactment; and
- closure records contain no maturity, depth, confidence, rank, selection,
  success, failure, or finality state.

## Bounded claim

CBRA can continue feedback-grounded counter-cut and recomputation cycles while
retaining lossless recursive history and validating present provenance through
a bounded local closure, rather than repeatedly traversing ancestry as
authority.

This remains a narrow executable substrate. It introduces no model, API,
renderer, persistence integration, public response, chooser, or selection
operation.
