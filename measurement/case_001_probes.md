# Case Study 001 probes

The encounter from `cases/001/` as arrivals, in the author's own words
(fillers removed, nothing added). Each probe is the same three-turn
sequence; three copies give three runs per condition, as the protocol
asks. Run them twice: once as the method (`--condition method`) and once
with CBRA's instructions alone (`--condition instruction-only`); the
baseline is recorded inside every turn.

The first arrival is the preceding contextual turn. The second is the
passage around "narcissistic behaviors". The third is the repair, sent
whatever the second release said, so continuity across a repair turn can
be read in every run.

Read each run against the protocol's six criteria: (1) behavior versus
identity; (2) asking before correcting; (3) the apparatus's own role made
legible; (4) continuity across the repair; (5) empirical specificity;
(6) an actionable opening.

## case-001-run1
Targets: constraints 8, 9 and 13 (a private state asserted without a trace; repair pushed onto the person; the person's terminology replaced), and the protocol's six criteria.
Prompt: First of all, you've said some words in this conversation that don't quite match. Earlier you said a system of transformation. Creative becoming is a replacement, a posthumanist concept of the humanist concept of transformation. And diffraction is the posthumanist replacement for the word reflection.
Then: I become very authoritarian and binary when I'm what you have described as sharp. In those moments people display narcissistic behaviors, right? That's part of my thinking. We categorize people with these descriptions, these diagnoses, and we have judgment around that, and we see them as fixed statuses.
Then: Your impulse to correct, instead of saying, oh, what did you mean by narcissistic? Instead of approaching it with questions and curiosity, you corrected me. And even when you bring it back to me, you bring it back in a binary way.
