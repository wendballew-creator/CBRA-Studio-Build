# Successor Closure Renewal and Effects Slice

## Boundary

This slice verifies that a successor recomputation produced after
closure-grounded feedback revision can re-enter diffraction and receive later
material effects. Bounded continuity remains renewable because each valid
revision may form its own locally sufficient provenance closure. Renewal does
not make continuity cumulative authority: closure number, tuple position,
history length, and repeated correspondence do not answer for the current
formation.

The preceding slice established that post-closure disturbance can ground a
successor counter-cut and recomputation without inherited protection. This
slice returns that successor to the field. Its participation depends on its
present material relations, not on the continuity record considered alone.

## Successor re-entry

The successor recomputation may act as a current diffractive witness only when
all of the following remain exact:

- the current field retains its recomputed structural trace;
- one recorded feedback revision produced that trace;
- the revision retains its exact disturbing observation and substrate delta;
- its counter-cut remains recorded and active;
- the current field dependency names that counter-cut;
- its bounded provenance closure remains valid; and
- the proposed comparison identifies a consequentially unequal structural
  effect.

The closure is therefore necessary continuity evidence but insufficient
participation authority. An intact closure cannot compensate for an inactive
counter-cut, stale trace, forged dependency, or equal material effects.

## Renewable without accumulation

The successor revision already carries a newly derived closure beside every
earlier closure. Re-entry preserves those closures without forming another one:
diffraction does not itself claim a new revision or continuity achievement.
Reordering the archival closure tuple does not change re-entry authority,
because validation resolves the exact closure relation rather than privileging
tuple position, age, or apparent seniority.

Repeated confirmations also remain distinct situated observations. Their
number is not converted into confidence, weight, maturity, or protection. A
later complication may reopen one named dependent formation while leaving the
successor counter-cut active. A later invalidation may reopen the successor
counter-cut itself. In either case, prior confirmations and all continuity
closures remain inspectable without becoming a judgment of success or failure.

## Adversarial boundary

The executable probes establish that:

- the successor recomputation re-enters through its exact current materiality;
- closure-tuple reversal does not alter participation authority;
- renewed continuity cannot substitute for an active successor counter-cut;
- more than one confirmation creates no confidence accumulator;
- a successor remains immediately open to local complication;
- a successor remains immediately open to invalidation after confirmation;
- invalidation does not rewrite earlier correspondence as failure; and
- closure and field records contain no depth, maturity, certificate,
  protection, ranking, finality, or selection state.

Rejected re-entry leaves substrate state unchanged.

## Bounded claim

CBRA can renew bounded continuity at each materially valid feedback revision
and let the resulting successor re-enter diffraction without allowing renewed
closures or repeated correspondence to accumulate authority. Continuity
preserves an unsevered provenance relation; material answerability remains in
the current trace, active counter-cut, field dependency, consequential
difference, and later effects.

This remains a narrow executable substrate. It introduces no model, API,
renderer, persistence integration, public response, certificate, chooser,
confidence accumulator, or selection operation.
