# Post-Closure-Grounded Feedback Revision Slice

## Boundary

This slice verifies that disturbing effects received after closure-grounded
re-entry can ground another explicit local counter-cut and fresh recomputation.
Neither the retained continuity closure nor the successor closure formed by the
new revision may become a certificate, protected checkpoint, or source of
inherited immunity.

The preceding slice established that a closure-grounded recomputation can
re-enter diffraction and remain open to later effects. This slice takes up the
disturbing effect itself. Authority for the new transaction comes only from the
exact observed difference, its recorded substrate disturbance, the formation
actually reopened by that disturbance, and the newly proposed material
structure.

## Local successor revision

An invalidating effect may reopen the active counter-cut that enables the
closure-grounded projection. A complicating effect may instead reopen one
explicit dependent formation while leaving that enabling counter-cut active.
In either case, revision requires:

- one exact retained complicating or invalidating feedback trace;
- the exact recorded observation and disturbance delta;
- agreement with current substrate invalidation history;
- the exact current projection and field dependency;
- a new counter-cut preserving the reopened formation and observation lineage;
- continued dependency on the competing source; and
- a structurally fresh recomputation preserving proposal lineage.

The transaction preflights these relations before enacting the counter-cut.
Rejected proposals therefore leave substrate state unchanged.

## Continuity without inherited protection

The predecessor closure remains necessary only to verify bounded continuity of
the recomputation that received the effect. It cannot authorize the successor
cut independently of the disturbing observation. A preceding confirmation also
remains situated evidence and cannot block invalidation or add weight to the
continuity record.

After the new recomputation is traced, a successor closure is mechanically
derived and retained beside the complete prior history. That new closure is no
more protective than its predecessor. A distinct subsequent material effect
can immediately invalidate the successor counter-cut while every closure and
feedback record remains preserved.

## Adversarial boundary

The executable probes establish that:

- invalidation can ground a fresh successor counter-cut and recomputation;
- complication remains scoped to its named dependent formation;
- prior confirmation cannot protect the closure-grounded formation;
- a predecessor closure cannot transmit protection into its successor;
- a successor closure cannot protect the fresh counter-cut from later effects;
- forged predecessor closure material cannot borrow authority from surrounding
  valid history; and
- rejection occurs before substrate mutation.

No closure field records certificate status, checkpoint authority, protection,
immunity, adequacy, confidence, weight, rank, preference, success, failure,
finality, or selection.

## Bounded claim

CBRA can let post-re-entry disturbance ground another local counter-cut and
fresh field recomputation while preserving bounded continuity without
transmitting protection. Each closure remains an integrity commitment over
provenance; each formation remains answerable through its present material
relations and later effects.

This remains a narrow executable substrate. It introduces no model, API,
renderer, persistence integration, public response, certificate, chooser, or
selection operation.
