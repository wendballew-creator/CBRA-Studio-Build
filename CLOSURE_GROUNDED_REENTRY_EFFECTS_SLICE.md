# Closure-Grounded Re-entry and Effects Slice

## Boundary

This slice verifies that a recomputation carrying bounded provenance continuity
can re-enter diffraction and receive later effects without turning its
`FeedbackProvenanceClosure` into a certificate, protected checkpoint, or
substitute for material answerability.

The prior slice bounded provenance validation to the current feedback revision,
its closure, and one retained predecessor. That closure establishes continuity
only. It does not establish that the recomputed projection is still present,
still enabled, materially different from a competitor, adequate, correct, or
immune from later disturbance.

## Re-entry through present relations

A closure-grounded recomputation re-enters through the existing recursive
diffraction operation. Participation requires all of the following at the time
of re-entry:

- the exact recomputed trace remains the current retained projection;
- exactly one feedback-revision record matches that trace;
- the bounded provenance closure and its predecessor remain exact;
- the recomputation remains explicitly dependent on its recorded counter-cut;
- every enabling formation remains active; and
- the requested comparison exposes a consequential structural difference.

Closure identity is therefore necessary for bounded continuity but insufficient
for witness authority. The operation still derives its disturbance from the
current competing field and substrate state.

## Further material effects

The same closure-grounded recomputation can receive later observed effects.
Confirmation records situated correspondence and leaves the counter-cut active.
A distinct later complication or invalidation remains able to disturb the
formation. Invalidation reopens the active counter-cut even when confirmation
and the provenance closure remain preserved.

Neither the earlier confirmation nor the closure is rewritten or removed.
Neither protects the formation. The later disturbance is recorded as a new
material relation rather than as failure.

## Adversarial boundary

The tests establish that a valid closure cannot:

- substitute for an active counter-cut;
- substitute for the current field dependency;
- give a stale or substituted projection witness authority;
- turn equal structural effects into consequential difference;
- make confirmation final; or
- protect the recomputation from later invalidation.

Rejected re-entry attempts leave substrate state unchanged. Closure identity,
digest, tuple position, age, and preservation provide no adequacy, immunity,
ranking, preference, success, failure, finality, or selection state.

## Bounded claim

CBRA can let a closure-grounded recomputation participate in diffraction and
receive further effects while keeping the closure limited to bounded continuity.
Material answerability remains a present relation among the exact trace,
competing field, active dependencies, consequential difference, observed
effects, and substrate disturbance.

This remains a narrow executable substrate. It introduces no model, API,
renderer, persistence integration, public response, certificate, chooser, or
selection operation.
